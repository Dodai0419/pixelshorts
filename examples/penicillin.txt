# Alexander Fleming, St Mary's Hospital, London, September 1928. Nobel Prize shared in 1945.
title: The Messy Desk That Saved Millions
lab day flash: One of the greatest medicines ever found started as a dirty dish.
town day walk: It was London in 1928, and Alexander Fleming was back from his summer holiday.
lab golden stand: His desk was famous for its mess, with old dishes stacked up by the window.
lab day talk: He started throwing them out, one at a time, talking with a colleague.
lab day stand: Then he stopped. One dish had grown a patch of fuzzy blue green mold.
lab day shake: All around that mold, the deadly bacteria he had been growing were simply gone.
lab golden talk: Most people would have binned it. Fleming said, that's funny, and he kept it.
lab night walk: He called the mold juice penicillin, but he could never make enough of it.
town night stand: Luckily, he published what he saw, even though almost nobody read it.
lab night talk: Unluckily, it then sat in a journal for about ten years, doing nothing.
lab day march: In Oxford, Howard Florey and Ernst Chain finally found the paper and built a team.
field day stand: Think of a weed in a garden that poisons its neighbors to keep the soil for itself.
lab golden boom: The mold had been doing that for millions of years. We just borrowed the trick.
town day crowd: By the end of the war, penicillin was saving soldiers by the thousands.
town golden stand: In 1945 the three men shared a Nobel Prize, all thanks to a dirty dish.
